I am an Assistant Professor in the Department of Civil Engineering at National Taiwan University (NTU), where I lead research on structural control, shake table dynamics, system identification, and experimental technology for earthquake engineering.

I received my Ph.D. in Structural Engineering from the University of California San Diego (UCSD) in 2024, advised by Professor Joel P. Conte. My doctoral research developed a comprehensive dynamic model — a digital twin — of the NHERI@UCSD Large High-Performance Outdoor Shake Table (LHPOST6), the world’s largest outdoor shake table, under both bare and loaded table conditions. I also hold an M.S. in Mechanical and Aerospace Engineering from UCSD and an M.S. in Structural Engineering from NTU.

Before my Ph.D., I worked at the National Center for Research on Earthquake Engineering (NCREE) in Taiwan, where I served as the first-generation chief operator of the 8m×8m 6-DOF shake table system at the NCREE Tainan Laboratory and helped procure a 2m×2m Stewart-platform shake table system.

My current research is supported by the Yushan Young Scholar Program (Ministry of Education, Taiwan) and includes a project on applying active control technology to the Taipei 101 wind damper system (National Science and Technology Council, Taiwan). I am always looking for motivated students interested in structural control, robotics, and experimental methods — feel free to reach out.
